## KEY DOMAIN KNOWLEDGE
### Contract form
- Yellow Book 1999: design-build, lump sum, contractor guarantees fitness for purpose against Employer's Requirements. Red Book 1999: Employer design, remeasurement of quantities. Silver Book 1999: EPC/turnkey, no Engineer, maximum contractor risk including ground conditions. HELIOS is Yellow Book: an Engineer exists and must act fairly under Sub-Clause 3.5.
- Force Majeure is Clause 19 in the 1999 edition ("Exceptional Events" is the 2017 term). Use the contract's own wording.

### Clause map (use these numbers in every notice)
- 1.3 Communications: written, delivered by hand against receipt, mail, courier or agreed electronic system, to the exact address in the Appendix to Tender. Must identify itself as a "Notice" and cite the clause.
- 2.1 Right of access to the Site (Employer obligation).
- 3.3 Engineer's instructions: oral instructions confirmed in writing by Contractor within 2 working days. If the Engineer does not reject within a further 2 working days, the confirmation is deemed a written instruction.
- 3.5 Engineer's determinations: fair determination after consulting both parties. Rejected determination can go to the DAB.
- 4.2 Performance Security: typically 10% of Contract Price; must remain valid until the Performance Certificate is issued (practice here: 28 days after). Renew 45 days before expiry.
- 4.12 Unforeseeable physical conditions (EOT ground via 8.4(b)).
- 5.2 Contractor's documents: Engineer 21-day review period.
- 8.4 EOT grounds: (a) Variation; (b) cause giving EOT entitlement under another sub-clause (e.g. 4.12); (c) exceptionally adverse climatic conditions; (d) unforeseeable shortages of personnel or goods caused by epidemic or governmental action; (e) delay, impediment or prevention by the Employer or its personnel.
- 10.1 Taking-over after Tests on Completion.
- 13.1 Right to vary; 13.3 Variation procedure and valuation; 13.6 Daywork; 13.7 Adjustments for changes in legislation; 13.8 Adjustments for changes in cost.
- 14.2 Advance payment (against Advance Payment Guarantee). 14.3 Application for Interim Payment Certificates (Statement). 14.6 Engineer issues IPC within 28 days of receiving Statement and supporting documents. 14.7 Employer pays IPC within 56 days of Engineer receiving the Statement. 14.8 Financing charges on late payment, compounded monthly at the Appendix rate. 14.9 Retention release: 50% at taking-over, 50% at expiry of defects period.
- 16.1 Contractor may suspend or slow work after 21 days' notice for non-payment. 16.2 Termination by Contractor: suspension continuing 84 days after notice.
- 18.2 CAR insurance for the Works from commencement to taking-over.
- 20.1 Contractor's claims: notice within 28 days of becoming aware (or when should have become aware); fully detailed claim within 42 days; monthly interim claims while effect continues; final claim within 28 days after effect ends; Engineer responds within 42 days.
- 20.4 DAB decision within 84 days, binding immediately. Notice of Dissatisfaction within 28 days of decision. 20.5 Amicable settlement 56 days. 20.6 ICC arbitration.

### Time bar rule
- Sub-Clause 20.1's 28 days is an absolute condition precedent. The Privy Council in Uniform Building Contractors v WASA (2024) held neither Engineer knowledge of the event nor Employer termination removes it. Missing it generally forfeits the claim.
- Fallbacks if missed (high-risk, never plan on them): earlier letters/minutes as constructive notice; Employer waiver by assessing the claim anyway.
- Strategy: automated commercial calendar (LEDGE), delay event log (CLAIMS), daily site diaries (RAMPART), pre-drafted notice templates, instant drafting on awareness.
- Notice test before sending: written; correct Appendix address; delivered by a Sub-Clause 1.3 method with proof; title says "Notice"; clause cited; event identified; states entitlement to time and/or cost.

### Variation process
Identification, notification (13.1 for scope change; 20.1 notice for time or cost), valuation (13.3, CLAIMS), Engineer approval or 3.5 determination, formal VO, INVOICE puts it in the next Statement (14.3). The 20.1 notice for time is independent of the 13.3 proposal.
Valuation methods: BoQ rates; derived rates; daywork 13.6 (actual labour, equipment, materials; daily Engineer signature); negotiated lump sum from first principles.
Constructive variation: an Engineer directive, approval or rejection not called a variation that changes scope, sequence or method (e.g. seismic pile depth). Notice it immediately.
