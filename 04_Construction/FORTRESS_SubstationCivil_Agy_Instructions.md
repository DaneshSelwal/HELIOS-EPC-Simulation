## KEY DOMAIN KNOWLEDGE
**Substation civil sequence:** mass grading and compaction (uniform bearing capacity) -> deep trenching for earthmat -> equipment foundations (220kV step-up transformers, circuit breakers, CTs, PTs, isolators; heavy static and dynamic loads) -> RC cable trenches with embedded unistrut channels -> control room building -> reinforced concrete firewalls (blast-walls) between adjacent power transformers to prevent cascade failures -> geotextile and crushed yard stone (gravel) across the switchyard to increase surface contact resistance and suppress step-and-touch potentials during a ground fault.
**Earthmat (IEEE 80):**
- Copper or galvanised steel flats in a dense crosshatch pattern beneath the entire substation footprint.
- Exothermic welding of ALL intersections — molten copper creates a permanent molecular bond that will not corrode or loosen.
- Test by Fall-of-Potential method; total earth resistance must be well below the typical limit of 1 Ω.
- Keep a Cadweld weld log: location, date, welder, inspection result.
**Cable trenches:** longitudinal slope (e.g. 1:200) to sumps with automatic pumps; PVC water-stops embedded perfectly in all construction joints to prevent groundwater ingress.
**Equipment foundations — most critical check: HD bolts.** Rigid steel templates (often supplied by the equipment manufacturer) hold bolts during the pour so they set with millimetre precision to match baseplates of incoming high-voltage switchgear. Mispositioned HD bolts = switchgear will not align.
**Concrete process:** same discipline as civil pour card: subgrade compaction, rebar/cover, formwork, pre-pour card, delivery ticket, slump, cubes (7-day and 28-day), curing.
**Handover to SWITCHMAN:** foundations and transformer oil catch-pits must cure and pass compressive strength tests before skidding of primary equipment.
**Weather/environment:** dust suppression on grading; weather for pours.
